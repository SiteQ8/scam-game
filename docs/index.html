Why this game exists

Scam messages reach everyone here: a parcel fee, a blocked account, a prize, a caller who sounds official.

Warnings tell us to be careful, but they rarely show what careful looks like. That comes from seeing many examples and learning the small signs: an address that is almost right, the hurry, the request for a code.

So I made this game. Every card follows a trick that scammers really use, in the Gulf and around the world, with fictional names and links that lead nowhere.

It is free and open source, with no ads, and it never asks for your data. Play the daily case with your family, and if a message ever fools you, send it to me so it can become a card.

Questions

Is it free? Yes. There are no ads, no purchases and no subscription.

Does it need the internet? No. The cards, voices and music are all inside the app, so it plays anywhere, even on a plane.

What does it collect? Nothing. There is no account and no analytics, and your scores stay on your phone.

Are the messages real? They are written to look real and follow tricks that scammers really use, but every name, number and link is fictional. The links lead nowhere and the QR codes only say they are a training example.

Why are some cards genuine? Because real life is mixed. A game where everything is a scam would teach you to distrust your own bank, so every daily case holds at least three genuine cards.

Which languages and regions? Arabic and English. In the settings you can choose examples from the Gulf, from around the world, or both.

How is the daily case chosen? Everyone on the same edition gets the same ten cards on the same day, so you can compare results without spoiling the answers.

Can I check a message I received? Yes. The checker looks for common warning signs on your device. It cannot prove that a message is safe, so when in doubt, contact the sender through a number you already trust.

Who is it for? Anyone with a phone. Playing together makes it easy to enjoy with family or a class.

Is it open source? Yes. The code and every card are public on GitHub, so anyone can check them or suggest a new card.

A real scam reached me. What now? Don’t tap the link and don’t reply. If you shared card details or a code, call your bank on the number printed on your card, then report it to the authorities in your country.

Where can I get it? Play the full game in your browser now. The App Store and Google Play versions are coming soon.
