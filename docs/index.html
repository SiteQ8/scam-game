    An international number

    If you were not expecting a message from abroad, treat this number with care.

  2. 2
    Asks for a payment

    Unexpected fees, fines or charges sent by message are the most common scam hook.

  3. 3
    Pressure to act fast

    Deadlines and threats of suspension are there to stop you from checking first.

  4. 4
    An address stuffed with words

    Long addresses full of extra words and hyphens are built to look official.

  5. 5
    Trust words inside the address

    Words like secure, verify or pay in an address are decoration, not proof.

Why I made it

Scam messages reach everyone here: a parcel fee, a blocked account, a prize, a caller who sounds official. Warnings ask us to be careful, but careful comes from seeing many examples and learning the small signs.

Free, open and private

Every card follows a trick scammers really use, in the Arabian Gulf and around the world, with fictional names and links. The game is free and open source, has no ads and never asks for your data.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads, no purchases and no subscription.

Does it need the internet?

No. The cards, voices and music are all inside the app, so it plays anywhere, even on a plane.

What does it collect?

Nothing. There is no account and no analytics, and your scores stay on your phone.

Are the messages real?

They are written to look real and follow tricks that scammers really use, but every name, number and link is fictional. The links lead nowhere and the QR codes only say they are a training example.

Why are some cards genuine?

Because real life is mixed. A game where everything is a scam would teach you to distrust your own bank, so every daily case holds at least three genuine cards.

Which languages and regions?

Arabic and English. In the settings you can choose examples from the Arabian Gulf, from around the world, or both.

How is the daily case chosen?

Everyone on the same edition gets the same ten cards on the same day, so you can compare results without spoiling the answers.

Can I check a message I received?

Yes. The checker looks for common warning signs on your device. It cannot prove that a message is safe, so when in doubt, contact the sender through a number you already trust.

Who is it for?

Anyone with a phone. Playing together makes it easy to enjoy with family or a class.

Is it open source?

Yes. The code and every card are public on GitHub, so anyone can check them or suggest a new card.

A real scam reached me. What now?

Don’t tap the link and don’t reply. If you shared card details or a code, call your bank on the number printed on your card, then report it to the authorities in your country.

Where can I get it?

Play the full game in your browser now. The App Store and Google Play versions are coming soon.
